How to plan a short video before you record or generate it

27 November 2026 · Creator guide

A short video is easier to make when you decide what the viewer should understand before you open an editing app. A little planning helps prevent unclear messages, unnecessary scenes and repeated edits.

1. Choose one audience and one goal

Start by naming the person you want to reach. A product demonstration for a first-time buyer needs different details from a tutorial for an experienced user. Then choose one goal: explain a process, answer a question, introduce a product, or invite the viewer to take a specific next step. If the video tries to do all four, its message may become difficult to follow.

2. Write the central sentence

Summarize the whole video in one sentence. For example: “This guide shows a small shop owner how to prepare a product clip using a phone.” Use that sentence as a filter. If a scene does not support it, remove the scene or save it for another video.

3. Build a simple three-part script

  • Opening: State the question, problem or result that makes the video relevant.
  • Middle: Show two or three useful steps or pieces of evidence.
  • Ending: Summarize the point and give one clear next action.

Read the script aloud. Replace long phrases with everyday language and remove claims you cannot support.

4. Plan scenes before editing

Create a short shot list. For a product video, that might include a wide view, a close-up of the important detail, a demonstration and a final view. For a tutorial, show the starting screen, each important action and the completed result. Record each scene for a little longer than you think you need so you have room to trim it.

5. Make the video easy to watch on a phone

Keep the main subject near the centre of the frame, leave space around text, and use captions that are large enough to read on a small screen. Avoid placing essential words close to the edges where platform controls may cover them. Check that the audio is clear and that the video still makes sense with sound muted.

6. Check accuracy and rights

Confirm product details, prices, dates and instructions before publishing. Use footage, music, images and logos that you created, licensed or have permission to use. If a person appears in the video, obtain permission where appropriate. Do not use synthetic media to mislead viewers about real events or endorsements.

7. Review before publishing

Watch the final export from beginning to end on a phone. Check spelling, captions, audio levels, visual cuts and the destination of any links. Ask someone unfamiliar with the project to explain the video's main message after watching it. If they cannot, simplify the opening or the structure.

Practical checklist: one audience, one goal, one central sentence, a short script, a shot list, readable captions, licensed assets and a final phone review.
