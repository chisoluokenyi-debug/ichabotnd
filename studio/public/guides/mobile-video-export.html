How to export a video for mobile viewing

27 November 2026 · Mobile creator guide

A good export is not simply the largest file or the highest resolution available. The right settings depend on where the video will be watched, how much detail the source contains and how quickly viewers need it to load.

Choose the frame shape first

Vertical 9:16 is commonly used for full-screen short-form mobile video. Square 1:1 can work for feeds, while horizontal 16:9 is useful for tutorials, presentations and traditional video players. Decide before editing so you can frame the subject and place captions correctly.

Use the source quality as your guide

Exporting a low-resolution recording at 4K does not create real detail. If the original footage is 1080p, a 1080p export is often a sensible choice. Use 4K when the source is genuinely 4K and the publishing destination supports it. Larger files take longer to upload and use more data for viewers.

Understand frame rate

Match the export frame rate to the source when possible. Common choices include 24, 25, 30 and 60 frames per second. Higher frame rates can make fast movement look smoother, but they also increase file size and are not automatically better for every type of video.

Choose a compatible format

MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio is widely supported by phones, browsers and social platforms. Keep a high-quality master copy of important projects, then create a smaller platform-ready version for sharing.

Make captions readable

Use high contrast between text and its background, avoid very thin fonts, and keep captions away from screen edges. Preview the video on the smallest phone screen available to you. Check that captions do not cover faces, product details or important interface controls.

Check sound and colour

Listen through the complete export. Confirm that speech is not too quiet, music does not overpower narration and there are no abrupt changes in volume. Check that colours and brightness look acceptable on a phone, not only on an editing monitor.

Keep a platform-specific copy

Different platforms may apply their own compression and display controls. Keep your original project and a clean master export, then make a separate copy if a platform requires a different aspect ratio, duration or file size. Avoid repeatedly re-exporting an already compressed file.

Quick export checklist: correct aspect ratio, source-matched resolution and frame rate, MP4/H.264 where suitable, clear AAC audio, readable captions, and a full playback test on a phone.
