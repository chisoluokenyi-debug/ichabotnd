How to make clear and ethical voiceovers

27 November 2026 · Audio guide

A voiceover should help people understand a video, not compete with it. Clear writing, a quiet recording space and respectful use of voices make a bigger difference than complicated equipment.

Write for the ear

Spoken language is different from written language. Use short sentences, familiar words and one idea at a time. Read the script aloud before recording. If you run out of breath or stumble over a phrase, rewrite it. Put pronunciation notes beside unfamiliar names, technical terms and local place names.

Prepare the recording space

Choose a quiet room and reduce steady background noise such as fans or television. Soft furnishings can reduce echo. Keep the microphone at a consistent distance and slightly to the side of the mouth to reduce harsh bursts of air. Record a short test and listen with headphones before recording the full script.

Record in sections

Record one paragraph or thought at a time. Leave a brief pause before and after each take. This makes it easier to remove mistakes and adjust pacing. Keep the microphone position and room setup consistent so the sound does not change between sections.

Edit for natural pacing

Remove long accidental silences and repeated words, but keep natural pauses that help listeners understand the message. Keep background music lower than speech. Listen on headphones and on a phone speaker; a mix that sounds balanced on headphones can be difficult to understand on a small device.

Captions and accessibility

Provide accurate captions for viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing, people watching without sound, and people who are learning the language. Review automatic captions manually, especially names, numbers and technical terms.

Consent for synthetic or cloned voices

A person's voice is part of their identity. Do not clone, imitate or generate speech that sounds like a real person unless you have that person's informed permission and the rights needed for the intended use. Permission should explain what will be created, where it may be published and whether it may be reused. Do not create fake endorsements, deceptive statements or recordings that could make listeners believe someone said something they did not say.

Be transparent

When synthetic speech could reasonably confuse an audience about who is speaking, explain that the voice is generated. Keep a record of the source and permission for voices and other assets used in a project.

Before publishing: confirm the script, pronunciation, sound quality, captions, music rights and permission for every identifiable voice.
